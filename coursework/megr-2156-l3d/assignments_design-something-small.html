Design

I wasn't 100% sure what I wanted to design at first. So I decided to just make an outline of how much space I was allowed to work with on SolidWorks. Once I did that, I had some ideas come to mind for a little desk accessory for my room. I decided I wanted to make a block with my name and a cross above my name. I thought it would look pretty cool. However, after it was actually printed out I realized it didn't look the best. I was a little disappointed in how it came out/looked. So I decided to do something else completely. Design pt.2

Here is the true start to this assignment. I decided to design a cross, sort of a small desk accessory for my room and for me to keep on display. I started the assignment by opening SolidWorks. Once in SolidWorks I made sure to have the units set to IPS. Then I opened the Sketch tool, clicked on the Top Plane view for my sketch. I then clicked on the box icon to make a regular box (didn't worry about the dimensions yet). 

After the rectangle was made, I then clicked on the Smart Dimensions tab to change the size of the rectangle. Since this was the Top Plane view I knew what the dimensions had to be. I scaled the rectangle to width = 0.25in and the length = 1.5in. 

I wanted to add something else to my design instead of just a square base. To add some extra texture to it. So I used the Straight Slot tool to make a sort of Ellipse at the base. I added relations to the ellipse so that it would be lined up perfectly with the rectangular base. In doing so I clicked on add relation, then the center lines I created inside both shapes, added the relations as colinear so they would be on top of one another. 

Next I moved on to the sizing of the cross itself. I made a regular rectangle and made sure it was concentric with the size of the base. I then used smart dimensions to scale it to what I believed was a good size. 

It was then time to boss extrude the outline of the design. I clicked the boss extrude button, selected all the contours and executed it. I made it 0.25in tall. 

Since I wanted to add more dimension to my design I then moved on to Boss Extruding the ellipse. I made that 0.35in tall (from the surface). 

It was then time to move on to the first beam of the cross. I at first thought it might be cool to have rounded edges, however after looking at it closer I thought it didn't look right. So I went back, changed it back to the square shape and Boss Extruded it to be 1.5in tall. (See blow for both processes)

I still liked the shape of the cross on the first design I created originally. So I wanted to mimic it. I started a new sketch on the Front Plane by clicking on the front of the design. I added a small triangle to it and smart dimensioned the angle to be 165 degrees from the horizontal. 

After that was complete it was time to do an Extruded Cut in the beam of the cross using the outline of the triangle I made. 

Next it was time to create the horizontal sides on the right and left side of the cross. I did this by making a new sketch, clicked on the side of the pre made beam and selected the box icon once more. I made sure that there were relations with the new rectangle to the side of the beam. I used the smart dimension tool to ensure it would have the same height as the original vertical beam. 

Once that step was complete I moved on to Boss Extrude the new horizontal beam. Once in the boss extrude settings I made sure to click on the Direction 2 option. I did this so that I wouldn't have to try and line up a whole new sketch. I made the right side of the horizontal 0.36in, and the left 0.56in to make up for the space that the vertical beam is taking up. It looked even and the measurements lined up so I moved on. 

Took a second to admire the work.

I then moved on to creating the slants at the ends of the horizontal beam. I did this by making the triangles to have a 165 degree slant in them (off the vertical). 

I then Cut Extruded the triangles to make the slants in the horizontal beam. 

I executed that, and put the finished product in isometric view. 

I then saved the design as a STEP file so that I would be able to open it up in the PrusaSlicer to 3D print.

Preprocess

When the PrusaSlicer is first opened, this is what it looks like. From here I had to add the new STEP file. 

Adding the new STEP file to the software. Click on the box with the plus sign. Then find your STEP file and click Open. 

I was then asked to select the requested quality of the mesh, I chose High. 

This is what it looked like after I clicked OK and uploaded the correct file. 

The first thing I did was check the Scale of the design to make sure it was under the assignment requirements. It was, because I made sure the measurements were correct on SolidWorks. 

I then made sure the Print Settings were correct on the front page (I did this before changing the Infill, and Skirt) to ensure that I had the right printer selected in case I had the wrong one selected. 

I went over to the Print Settings tab, changed the Vertical Shells Perimeter to have a minimum of 4 (the default is 2). 

I also wanted to change the Infill. I changed the default density from 15% to 30%. To make the print a little more durable in case of a fall from my desk, but also to make it a little heavier so that a light touch wouldn't automatically knock it over. Next I changed the Fill pattern from Grid to Gyroid. Not only does this pattern look super cool, it is a great pattern for the print to be sturdy. Gyroid is a type of infill that makes the strength equal through the entire print, it is very durable, doesn't cross itself in the same layer, and uses less filament than other fill patterns. I also changed my wall thickness from 0.95mm to 1.84mm, this made the design more dense, and increased its strength. 

Next I moved on to the Skirt and brim. I changed the skirt loops to a minimum of 1. In doing this it makes sure the printer is heated up and ready to go before starting the print of my design. I also changed the Brim to an Outer and inner brim, with a width of 0.8mm. This will help with my design when it comes to making all those corners look good. (See image below to see what exactly a skirt an brim is, skirt is the outline not connected to the design, the brim is the outline that connects to the base of the start of the print)

Once everything in your settings is complete it is time to click the Slice Now button. Once clicked you will be able to see your custom settings. Below you can see the vertical shells that go around the parameter of the design (the yellow). I could also see the Gyroid infill (the red). As well as the skirt and brim around the outside of the design (green).

Once everything looks good it is time to upload the file on to the flash drive (from the printer). 

Save the file to your personal computer first (for future reference). Then repeat the process and upload it to the flash drive.

Research

There are a total of 24+ infills offered on the PursaSlicer software. I will be going over Rectilinear, Line, and Honeycomb infills. The Rectilinear is one of the fastest printing infills and is only really recommended when doing a 100% infill density. The Rectilinear is a lot like the Grid infill except, the Rectilinear has double the support compared to Grid while using the same amount of material as Grid. The Line infill is a lot like the Rectilinear infill, except it is printed at acute angles. This infill also doesn't have any crossing paths in one layer. The Line infill is very durable, and uses around the same amount of material as Rectilinear. Finally we have the Honeycomb infill, this one is so incredible cool looking (like Gyroid). The Honeycomb has small gaps in its layers. It is very sturdy overall, however the downside of this infill is that it is on of the longest to print when looking at print time. Since I already discussed the Gyroid infill earlier in my documentation I thought it would be good to look at some more. Overall my favorite/the one I find coolest is the Gyroid infill. 

Things to Change

  • I would change the base of the design to look even more aesthetically pleasing.
  • Next time I will consider how fonts will print incorrectly when there are gaps/no supports, for example on my first print the letters were all wonky because there were just gaps/holes where the letters didn't print correctly. 
  • I will change the print density % to make a faster print when it comes to small prints, at least when it is something that doesn't need to be super durable. 
  • I want to change the corners to be more rounded to see how the print results differently when there are sharp corners. 

Lessons Learned

  • I learned that you can save the STEP file off SolidWorks using either of the two options and they will both work.
  • I learned that when you try and Boss Extrude/Cut Extrude a font, you will have to create a seperate sketch in order for the software to recognize it. 
  • I learned that when you over define a sketch it is a lot harder to fix, than if you wait to add relations toward the end of the design. 
  • To always remember to change the units to whatever you're wanting them to be, before starting anything on SolidWorks! I forgot and had to redo all the smart dimensions the first time.
