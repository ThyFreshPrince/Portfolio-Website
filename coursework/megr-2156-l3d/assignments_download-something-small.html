MEGR 2156 L3D

Download

Downloaded from nasa keychain.stl from https://www.printables.com/model/836761-nasa-worm-logo-keychain/files Links to an external site. (see website below). NASA is one of the coolest aerospace companies in the world. I have always thought it would be amazing to work for NASA one day. They were the first space agency who successfully the first humans to walk on the moon. They continually inspire people everyday with their great accomplishments. I believe they are one of, if not every engineer's inspiration. I thought the keychain would be a great accessory for my backpack. 

Before I settled on the NASA keychain, I also considered printing either a Minecraft or STARWARS keychain(See below). However since we could only use the black print material I decided against it since I knew it would not look the same as when you have more colors to choose from. 

Preprocessor

I opened the file in the Prusa Slicer, then realized the keychain was too big for the assignment requirements. The x and y sizes were 3.1in and 0.80in. I had to choose a different build orientation in order for it to be under the assignment requirements. 

 

I then had to change the settings, since the print was so small I changed the print settings to 0.25mm to make it a little more dense/increase its thickness. The filament was on the wrong setting so I put it on the Generic PLA. The printer was also on the wrong default, so I had to choose the Original Prusa MK4s HF0.4 nozzle which is the correct printer type. The final settings before slicing.

Since the print was too big, I had to scale the x and y down to 2in and 0.5in. I had to scale it down to meet the assignment requirements. I did this by first changing the scale in the x axis which automatically scaled the y and z axis to their correct measurements. 

Now it looks like the picture below.

Clicked the “Slice Now” button and used the default settings. The print did not require supports.

Clicked the “Export G-Code”.

Then I clicked “Save”.

Print

Me and John then went over to the 3D Printer, printer number 1 in the Fab Lab, and took a flash drive to upload the file to it. 

Then plugged the flash drive into my computer and uploaded it. Ejected the flash drive, and then plugged it back into the 3D printer.

There is a prompt on the screen where it shows the print designs on the display, as well as the file name.

The display shows the print times, and material used. The display also shows the temperature of the bed, and the nozzle temperature. Once I confirmed the files and print, I pushed the button to execute the print however could also use the touch screen to execute the print.

Once the print is executed, the print initiates. The display shows the expected time as well as the percent of completion. Then a red light appeared on the print to display the initiation.

It starts to calibrate and get everything warmed up.

Then the print test strip/area gets printed.

The print starts the process after the print area gets laid down. Then the print process starts the first layer. 

Then the percentage moves up to start the initiation.

Then the printer keeps layering more material down. Video of the printing process (cc. Pedro's group video).

Then the print is complete, and the percentage says 100%.

Once complete, look over the prints and confirm the prints are officially complete.

Once the items are correct, then bend the print bed and remove the prints. Below image is the front of the print.

Image of the final print, the back of the print is smooth while the front is a little ridged/textured from the print.

Lessons Learned

  • There are hundreds of keychains to use on printables. 
  • Realized that the sliced info also gave you an estimated cost for the print, as well as the estimated print time for your design. 
    • Normal Mode
    • Stealth Mode
  • When you input a change in the size with relation to the x axis for example, the design automatically changes the y and z measurements to ensure the print is symmetrical to the original design. 
  • You can use the touch screen or the button on the 3d printers display. 
  • When you save/download the sliced g-code from PrusaSlicer you can actually save the file straight to the flash drive without exporting it to your computer first. 
  • When handling the beds or trays for the print to print on, be sure to not put your hands/fingers all over it because it can soak in the oils from your hands which will ruin the bed over time. So grip it by the sides. 
  • John was kind enough to help me with the downloading, and printing process. Pedro and his group were also kind enough to share with me the video they took of their printing process. I didn't realize when I printed mine that the video was a requirement for the documentation process. I will be more thorough next time to make sure I get everything I need for it. 
  • The resources used were the Printables website, and the PrusaSlicer software. 
  • The amount of time used for this assignment was 3-4 hours.
