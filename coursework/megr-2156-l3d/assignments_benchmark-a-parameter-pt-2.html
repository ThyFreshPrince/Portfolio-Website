Benchmark a Parameter pt. 2

In the end, thankfully everything failed exactly where I was expecting it to. The bridge failed so much worse that I ever imagined. I was very surprised that it failed the way it did. Also the overhang failed, but not nearly as bad as the bridge. I believe if I were to have made the overhang come down a little more, that it would also have failed worse. I am happy to say that the outcome was not different than what I originally thought. This assignment was fun, and while I spent most of my time trying to think of what to design, specifically what I thought would be the most likely design to make the printer fail I was definitely happy with its outcome. 

Lessons Learned

As far as the lessons learned. I learned that when it comes to using 3D printers, supports are crucial for any sort of overhang or bridge. I think if it were one of my first times using a 3D printer, this would not have been something I realized right off the bat. This lesson is especially essential to remember when doing any sort of prototyping for future companies. This lesson will help me and others to remember to always check for any overhangs and/or any sections that need the extra support. Since this will help save time and material for the future. I learned that with some assignments its better to remember to keep things simple instead of always tying to do or come up with the coolest or most complex design. This lesson would have saved me time from not having to go back and simplify my design.  I also learned to make sure that the dimensions I am printing/designing will fit on the actual print tray/bed. That way I don't have to scale it again when I go to print it. Another lesson I learned is how strong the Cubic infill is for square/rectangular containers. My main tower, I used only 15% infill but since the infill was a Cubic fill type, I could squeeze the main tower as hard as I wanted and I never felt it budge. I was very impressed!
