A3: Parametric and FEA

Part 1:

The purpose of this assignment was to design a bar which has a hollow box cross section where I was only given the following criteria, the max deflection, the material, and the load. I had to choose my own dimensions for the hollow box bar's minimum geometry like the length, height, weight, and thickness. This was done through parametric design, while the bar is under direct compression. In the end I had to verify the geometry through finite element analysis (FEA). The criteria for this assignment was given as the following, the direct load had to be between 250lbf <  F < 500lbf. The maximum axial defection of the bar is 0.01 inches. The bar's material was to be made out of Gray Cast Iron or Ductile Cast Iron. Lastly the Young's Modulus of elasticity was given as a rang of (11.6 - 21.7) X 106. With that, it was time for me to start the assignment. The first thing I did was make a list of known, and unknowns(Box 1, and Box 2) as well as a rough sketch of what it would look like. I decided to make my outer Box1 base and height equal to values a = 5in and b = 5in. I then moved onto Box2 I made those values as c = 2.5in and d = 2.5in. With these values I decided it was time to use SolidWorks.

2157 Students Only - Modify Design Parameters

The assignment instructions are to cycle through part 2 of the assignment above and change the design parameters which include the load, thickness, height, and width. However I must keep the material and fixture the same. Before I calculate it I must take a guess on whether I believe the length will increase, decrease, or stay the same. It is funny, I did this already half way into this assignment earlier because I didn't like the way my original parameters looked in the SolidWorks, but I am happy to do it once more!

Since the equation for Length is given by L=(delta*A*E)/F I can already see that if I increase my Force the overall length will decrease. With that being said whenever I increase my height, and width that will only increase the area, in turn this will also make the length increase. For this activity I will increase my height and width, which will automatically increase my area and thickness. Next I will decrease my Force value, which in turn will also increase my overall length. I believe once I do this my overall length will be extremely long. Lets test out this guess with some new parameters. I opened SolidWorks, and I put these as my new parameters.

I changed my a and b values to a=7.25in and b=7.25in. I also changed the c and d values to c=3.5in and d=3.5in. Lastly I decided to make my Force = 250lb. Once all of those new values were in, my guess was correct. The new overall length came out to be 19,250in! That made the length increase a ton. This also increased my areas and thickness. My prediction was correct.
